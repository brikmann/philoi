# Discovery Interview Guide — "finding people for X" demand

*Goal: find out whether **"I want to do X (gym / run / study) with people but can't find them, or it's too intimidating to find them in person"** is a real, painful, common problem — WITHOUT mentioning Philoi. Testing the KEY INSIGHT from the master research doc.*

## Rules (solution-blind — read before every interview)

- **Never mention your app or any solution.** If they ask what you're building: *"I'm just trying to understand how people stay consistent — I'll show you what I'm working on after, I don't want to bias you."*
- **Ask about the past and the specific, not the hypothetical.** "Tell me about the last time…" beats "would you…". People lie about the future and tell the truth about the past.
- **Follow the emotion, then shut up.** When they light up or get frustrated, dig there. Silence is your friend.
- **Don't fish for validation.** No "wouldn't it be great if…". You're hunting for the truth, even if it kills the idea.

## Who to talk to

People trying to build a fitness/study habit. Get a **mix**: some who already have a group/friends they do it with, some who don't — the whole point is comparing whether the pain differs. (Gevorg-type = wants people for an activity but has no obvious way to find them = your prime target.)

## The questions

**Warm-up (their real behavior)**
1. What have you been trying to be more consistent with lately — gym, running, studying, something else? How's it actually going?
2. Walk me through the last time you tried to stick with it. What happened — where did it break down?

**Solo vs. together (don't lead)**
3. Do you do it alone or with other people? How did it end up that way?
4. (If alone) Have you ever *wished* you were doing it with someone? Tell me about a specific time you felt that.
5. The last time you wanted a gym buddy / running partner / study group — what did you actually *do* about it?
6. What made it hard to find someone? *(Listen for: don't know anyone into it, schedules never line up, don't know where to look, too awkward/intimidating.)*

**The intimidation probe (your specific hypothesis)**
7. Have you ever looked into a run club / class / group but not gone, or gone once and not stuck? What happened?
8. What actually goes through your head about showing up to a group of people you don't know? Which part is the hard part?
9. What would've had to be true for you to actually walk in and stay?

**Intensity & cost (is it a real pain or a mild one?)**
10. How much does *not* having people to do this with actually affect whether you stay consistent — honestly?
11. Have you ever spent money or real effort to solve this? (trainer, class pack, ClassPass, switched gyms, etc.)
12. When your own friends flake on it, what do you do instead?

**Wrap**
13. If you could wave a magic wand at the "doing this with people" part, what would it do?
14. Who's the person you know who has this problem *worse* than you? *(→ referral / next interview.)*

## What to record — signal vs. noise

- ✅ **Real signal:** unprompted past attempts to find people, money/effort already spent, visible emotion around intimidation or loneliness, concrete workarounds.
- ⚠️ **Weak signal (discount it):** "yeah that'd be cool," hypotheticals, future-tense enthusiasm, politeness.
- Capture **verbatim quotes.** Tag each person as **has-group vs. no-group**, and note whether the pain is sharper for the no-group people — that's the exact thing you're trying to confirm.

## Logistics

~15 min each. Record if they're okay with it. Log results into the Pre-Beta Feedback Log (or section 3 of the master research doc) after — quotes + the has-group/no-group tag.
