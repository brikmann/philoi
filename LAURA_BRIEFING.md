## TL;DR (the 60-second version)

1. **I didn't start with Philoi.** I started with Aspire OS / Cadence — a solo, wearable-driven behavior-change tool. I ran real discovery (29 people, then a structured 30×30 sprint).
2. **The discovery told me two things:** the behavior-consistency problem is real, but the *solo-utility* framing had weak pull ("ok, why do I care") — and independent voices kept asking for the same missing ingredient: **community, accountability, competition, "find your people."**
3. **That's the pivot to Philoi:** keep the problem (people can't stay consistent on their goals), change the mechanism from *solo physiology* to *social accountability* — circles of friends, photo check-ins, streaks, leaderboards.
4. **Now I'm testing it honestly:** interviews on the new concept, a live beta, a controlled "one circle live" retention test, and external demand research — against a defined funnel loop with real target numbers.

## Part 1 — How I found the problem existed

**Starting product:** Aspire OS (a.k.a. Cadence) — connect wearable + calendar, share your morning state, and it generates a daily "protocol": peak windows, crash windows, what to eat when, what to protect. Framed as a behavior-change intervention, not a productivity tool.

**Discovery, phase 1 (n≈29, five channels):** semi-structured interviews + event conversations + live-alpha signups. Method notes I can defend to Laura:

- Captured verbatim quotes, demographic, and willingness-to-pay per person.
- **Corrected my own biases in writing:** re-coded 3 family/friends from "positive" to "neutral"; flagged a $30/mo quote as an income outlier that can't anchor student pricing. (This is the honest-integrity move — I under-count rather than inflate.)
- Result after correction: **students the strongest segment (~70% positive), working professionals ~56%.** Two faculty *independently and unprompted* named students as the sharpest ICP.

**The signal that caused the pivot** — multiple independent people pointed at the same missing thing:

- *"Add community and keep people accountable."* — Badri
- *"They'd care about some competition aspect — connect with your friends."* — Joe Rideout
- *"Strava is huge for runners — find your people and work together."* — Chad Lebold (Laurier prof)
- A senior professional pushed on adding a *"Strava-style community layer."*
- *"A lot of information is put in front of them but it's like, ok, why do I care."* — Nabil (the solo-utility pull problem)
- *"Manually, people will just forget it after two weeks."* — Dr. Philip Mathew (solo tool = weak retention without integrations)

**The read:** the behavior-change value was real, but a solo tool had weak pull and weak retention. The pull people kept describing was *social*. So the problem to own isn't "optimize your physiology" — it's **"people can't stay consistent on their goals alone, and the thing that actually moves them is other people."**

## Part 2 — How I arrived at the solution (Philoi)

**The pivot:** same underlying problem (consistency/behavior change), new mechanism. From a solo physiology dashboard → **Philoi: social accountability in small circles.** You set a goal, you take a photo check-in, your circle sees it, streaks + leaderboards create friendly competition. It's the "community/accountability/Strava-for-goals" layer the Aspire OS research kept pointing to — made into the core, not a bolt-on.

**Why this is a defensible move, not a random pivot:** it's directly traceable to independent, unprompted signals in the prior research (above). I moved *toward* the thing multiple people described wanting, and *away* from the thing they bounced off ("why do I care," integration-dependence, solo-tool forgetting).

## Part 3 — What I'm actively doing to test it + prove demand

- **New-concept interviews (n≈16):** re-ran discovery on Philoi itself (question set below). Finding: everyone *gets* it; competitive friend-groups lean in; solo/self-motivated people don't. Sharpest insight — an **on-ramp mismatch**: the model asks you to bring your own circle, which serves people who least need it (tight groups already coordinating in a chat) and misses those who'd value it most (people with no group).
- **Competitive landscape:** mapped it honestly. Direct clones exist (Folksable, FistBump), Strava owns the fitness-social comparison, and the group chat is the invisible default. → format isn't the moat; the wedge is a specific segment + solving cold-start.
- **Live beta + activation:** first cold link-send got zero (activation friction, since diagnosed + fixed). Warm, hands-on conversion works — got real users on, and one tester pulled a friend into his circle in person (first organic growth). One cold-ish IG follower (same gym, no relationship) downloaded on his own — my cleanest demand signal.
- **The retention test (running now):** get one full circle live in a single sitting, then measure whether it sustains *without me poking it* over 7 days. This is the real test of whether the core loop holds.
- **External demand research:** validated that "want accountability but have no one" is a real, monetized problem (whole category of gym-buddy finder apps; Focusmate proves people accept accountability from strangers; run clubs booming but "most solo runners never join one"). Parked as the next hypothesis to test, not yet committed.

## The question sets (what Laura asked to see)

### Set A — "Between Aspire OS and Philoi": the 30×30 discovery sprint (solution-blind)

*Advisor-prescribed depth: 30 students × 30 min. Semi-structured, open-ended, never yes/no — designed to surface real pain, not validate a pitch. Six phases:*

**Phase 1 — Opening + context**
- Tell me about your program and where you are in school.
- What does a typical week actually look like for you right now?
- Living on campus, off-campus with roommates, or at home?

**Phase 2 — Time + schedule reality**
- Walk me through your last big assignment/exam week, hour by hour. What did it *actually* look like?
- When do you feel sharpest? When are you running on fumes?
- Tell me about the last time you said "I'm going to do X today" and then didn't. What happened?
- What's the gap between when you *wanted* to be productive last week and when you actually were?
- Where does your time disappear to that you can't account for?

**Phase 3 — Energy + body breakdowns**
- Tell me about a recent time your body got in the way of what you wanted to do (sickness, exhaustion, a crash).
- How does your sleep change during midterms/finals vs. now?
- What do you do when you're depleted but still need to perform?
- If you had one extra hour of clarity a day, where would you put it?
- What's the price you pay — academically, socially, physically — for how you currently manage your energy?

**Phase 4 — Current tools + workarounds**
- What apps/tools do you *actually* use to manage time, energy, or health?
- What have you tried and given up on — and why? (the "friction graveyard")
- Walk me through the last time you opened your Health/Watch app. What did you do with that info?
- Most useful thing your phone does for you? Least useful?
- One tool that genuinely changed how you manage your week — what and why?

**Phase 5 — Demo + reaction**
- Looking at this, what do you notice first?
- What's confusing? What's missing?
- If you used this every morning for four weeks, what would have to be true for it to stick?
- When in your day would you actually open this?
- Who'd benefit most from this, and who least? Why?

**Phase 6 — Hypothetical + conversion + referral**
- If this saved you 30 min of friction every morning during finals — most you'd reasonably pay/month? Least you'd pay and still take it seriously? *(two-anchor pricing — no anchoring bias)*
- If this disappeared tomorrow, what would you miss most? *(Sean Ellis PMF test)*
- What would have to be true for you to recommend it to a friend?
- Can I send you the link to try it free for four weeks?
- Who else in your network has this problem? Mind introducing me to two?

### Set B — Philoi feedback question set (testing the new solution)

*Used on the ~16 Philoi-concept interviews. Structured A–H:*

- **A. First impression** — what do you think it does? gut reaction 1–5 + why? one-line pitch to a friend?
- **B. Core loop** — is the loop (goal → photo check-in → circle sees + streak) motivating? does photo proof make you more likely to do it? friends seeing you — motivating or stressful?
- **C. Goals & fit** — which goals would you track? social-media/screen-time goal useful? missing goal type?
- **D. Circles & social** — would you start/join a circle with friends? who? does "own goals but in it together" click?
- **E. Ranks & competition** — tiers fun / motivating / cringe? leaderboards keep you in or turn you off?
- **F. Intent & virality** — use it for a week if friends were on? how many friends could you pull in? beta tester?
- **G. Friction & value** — confusing / annoying / missing? what would stop you using it? pay a few $ for extras?
- **H. Open** — one thing that would make it a must-use?

*The evolution to show Laura: Set A is solution-blind and hunts for the problem; Set B tests a specific solution's loop, social pull, and virality. Both refuse yes/no and both end on referral/must-use.*

## Reasonable funnel-loop goals

The Philoi loop, stage by stage, with **honest early-beta targets** — the numbers I'd consider real signal at this stage (small n, so these are directional gates, not statistics). Split into "minimum viable signal" vs. "strong signal."

| Loop stage | What it measures | Min viable signal | Strong signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Invite → signup** | warm-conversion friction | 60% | 80%+ |
| **2. Signup → in a circle** | do they join/create | 70% | 90% |
| **3. Circle → goal set** | activation step | 80% | 95% |
| **4. Goal → first check-in** | *activation complete* | 60% | 80% |
| **5. D1 return check-in** | comes back day 2 *unprompted* | 40% | 55%+ |
| **6. D7 still checking in** | early habit | 25% | 40%+ |
| **7. Social response rate** | % of check-ins that get a reaction/comment | 50% | 70%+ |
| **8. Circle liveness** | circle active 7 days *without me poking* | 1 of 1 circles | multiple |
| **9. Viral k-factor** | friends each user pulls in who join | 0.3–0.5 | ≥1.0 (self-sustaining) |

**How to read these for Laura:**

- Stages 1–4 are **activation.** My cold send failed here (fixed now); warm conversion is already clearing the bar.
- Stages 5–8 are the **core loop / retention** — the real test, running this week via the single-circle experiment. *This is the number that decides whether the product works.*
- Stage 9 is the **growth loop.** k ≥ 1 is the long-term "it spreads on its own" goal; I'm not there and not claiming to be.
- Benchmark context (consumer social rule-of-thumb): ~D1 40% / D7 20% / D30 10% is a common bar. Inside a *live circle* I should beat that, because the accountability is the point — if I don't, that's a real finding, not spin.

**Current honest state:** activation fixed and converting warm; retention + social-response + virality all **unproven** and being measured now. I'm reporting the gates *before* the data so the test is falsifiable.

## How I'm testing retention (quantified)

*This is the specific, falsifiable test — stated with pass/fail numbers before the data comes in.*

**The setup:** one full circle onboarded together in a single sitting (first check-ins done that night), then observed for **7 days with zero founder intervention** — no reminders, no nudges, no poking.

**Pass bars — retention counts as "proven" only if all four hold:**

1. **Circle survives 7 days without me poking it** — activity sustains on its own, not because I prompted it.
2. **D1 return ≥ 40%** — at least 40% of members post a check-in the day *after* onboarding, unprompted.
3. **D7 return ≥ 25%** — at least 25% are still checking in on day 7.
4. **Social-response rate > 50%** — more than half of all check-ins get a reaction or comment from a circle-mate (proves it's a *social* loop, not a solo tracker).

**Fail condition (stated up front):** if a fully-live circle misses these, it's a **value / ICP problem, not an activation problem** — and the honest next move is back to segment discovery to find higher-intensity users, *not* more building.

**Why these numbers:** consumer-social rule-of-thumb is ~D1 40% / D7 20%. Inside a live accountability circle I should *meet or beat* that — the whole premise is that friends + streaks lift retention above a solo app. If I can't beat the baseline when the accountability is the point, that's a real finding, not something to spin.
